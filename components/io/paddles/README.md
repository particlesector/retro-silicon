# Paddles

The Atari 2600's four paddle inputs, as the console's circuit works and Stella 7.0 models it. Each paddle is a 1 Mohm pot. On the console's board it charges a 68 nF capacitor from 5 V through itself and a 1.8 kohm resistor. The TIA reads the capacitor on INPT0-INPT3 as 1 once it passes a threshold, and while VBLANK bit 7 is set the TIA dumps all four to ground. A game dumps the capacitors, lets go, and counts the lines until its paddle's input reads 1: the further the knob is turned, the higher the resistance and the longer it takes, from under a line at one end to nearly 380 lines at the other.

Module `paddles`, with `paddles_rate` (each position's charge rate, generated by `tools/gen_rate.py`). The TIA has the dump and the input ports (`video/tia`'s `dump` and `pot_hi`); this component is everything outside the chip: the pots, resistors and capacitors.

## The model

Each capacitor's voltage `u`, a 40-bit fraction of 5 V, takes one step a colour clock, each rounded to the nearest:

- **dumped** (VBLANK bit 7 set): `u -= u * dump_rate`, through 50 ohm;
- **charging:** `u += (1 - u) * rate(pos)`, through the pot and 1.8 kohm;
- **no paddle plugged in:** `u` holds, as Stella leaves an unconnected pin (only a dump changes it, and from power-up it stays 0).

Each step is the exact solution of the RC circuit over one colour clock, `1 - exp(-1 / (R C f))` of the way to the target, with `f` Stella's colour clock rate (60 x 228 x 262 a second). `hi` (the TIA's input pin reading 1) is `u` above the threshold: the voltage a 1 Mohm pot reaches 379 lines after a dump, 0.298 of 5 V. A knob at position `p` is a pot of `p / 256` Mohm, so each position is one of Stella's (which are at multiples of 1/4096 Mohm), and the middle, 128, is Stella's default: from a full dump its input reads 1 after 43,284 colour clocks, about 190 lines.

The rates are a block RAM ROM (256 positions, 28 bits each: a 24-bit mantissa, whose top bit is always set and isn't stored, and a shift), looked up one paddle a clock in turn, so a new position takes effect within 6 clocks. One multiplier steps the four capacitors in turn, in two stages: a capacitor's product (41 x 24 bits) on one clock, its scaling, rounding and sum on the next. Each mantissa is within 2^-24 of the exact rate, and each step is rounded, so over the longest charge (1 Mohm) the crossing is within about 0.02 of a colour clock of the exact one.

## Timing

After each colour clock edge the four capacitors take that colour clock's step, with `dump` as it stands after the edge (the TIA's VBLANK bit 7, which a write changes on its own edge), over the next 5 clocks. So by the next edge `hi` is the capacitor at the end of the colour clock being read, and a read `3n` colour clocks after a dump ends sees `3n` steps of charging, as Stella's timestamps count them. `cc` must come at least 6 clocks apart. Each capacitor's operands (its voltage, its distance to 5 V and its rate) are registered a clock before its multiply, on the colour clock for the first and then one a clock, so the multiplier's inputs start at registers; `dump` picks between them at the multiply.

## Ports

| Port | Dir | Width | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `clk`, `rst` | in | 1 | Clock (block RAM read on both edges, below); synchronous reset: every capacitor empty |
| `cc` | in | 1 | One colour clock |
| `dump` | in | 1 | VBLANK bit 7 (`video/tia`'s `dump`) |
| `on` | in | 4 | Paddle i is plugged in (INPT0-3) |
| `pos` | in | 32 | Paddle i's knob in bits 8i+7:8i, a pot of `pos / 256` Mohm |
| `hi` | out | 4 | Input i's capacitor is above the TIA's threshold (`video/tia`'s `pot_hi`) |
| `st_addr`, `st_rdata`, `st_we`, `st_wdata` | | 3, 32, 1, 32 | Save-state port (below) |

The ROM's address is taken on the falling edge, as `audio/apu-2a03`'s mixer's is (on the GW2AR-18 a block RAM's address needs more hold than a short path from a register gives).

## Parameters

| Parameter | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SAVE_STATE` | 1 | 0 leaves out the save-state port's logic: writes do nothing and reads give 0 |

## Save state

The port follows [docs/conventions.md](../../../docs/conventions.md): 5 words, written while the capacitors aren't stepping (from the 6th clock after `cc`). Words 0-3 are each capacitor's `u`, its low 32 bits; word 4 their high bytes, paddle 0's in bits 7-0. The rates aren't saved: they follow `pos`.

## Status

Sim tests (`sim/paddles_test.cpp`) check `hi` on every colour clock against the circuit's equations, computed separately in double precision:
- **Charging:** from a full dump at positions 0, 1, 7, 64, 128, 200 and 255, 90,000 colour clocks each; the middle position reads 1 after exactly 43,284.
- **Partial dumps:** 300 rounds of a dump of 5 to 46 colour clocks, a new position (or none) for each paddle, and a charge of up to 60,000 colour clocks, each paddle's crossing to the colour clock.
- **Save state:** saved mid-charge and restored into another: the same `hi` for 40,000 colour clocks. Without save states (`SAVE_STATE = 0`) it charges the same.
- `paddles_rate` is what `tools/gen_rate.py` writes.

In `systems/atari-2600`, the `tia_paddles` test cartridge matches Stella's golden frame, which checks the colour clock each paddle crosses on, the reads before and in a dump, and all four inputs.
